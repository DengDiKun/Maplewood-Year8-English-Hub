Text A — Crossing the Stage

Behind the blue curtains, I waited to deliver my speech at the school showcase. The hall no longer felt familiar. The curtains were harbour gates, and beyond them stretched a dark sea of faces. Rows of parents murmured like distant waves. White stage lights swept across the floor, turning the polished boards into water. My cue card trembled in my hand.

Ms Liyana stood beside me and whispered, “Breathe. Find your first sentence.” Her calm voice was a lighthouse at the edge of my storm. I tried to follow its beam, but the audience's coughs and shifting chairs grew louder in my mind. For a moment, I wanted to remain safely on shore.

Then the announcer called my name. The harbour gates opened. I stepped onto the narrow deck of the stage, and every face seemed to rise like another wave. My first sentence disappeared. I looked down, found the words on my card and began again. In the front row, Aina gave me a small smile. It was a warm lamp in the darkness.

Slowly, my words found a steady rhythm. Each sentence carried me forward. I stopped staring at the whole sea and focused on one friendly face at a time. When I finished, the applause rose around me, not as a frightening storm, but as a warm tide. I smiled. At last, I had reached the other shore.

Word help: harbour = a safe place for boats • lighthouse = a tower whose light guides ships • tide = the regular movement of the sea

Text B — A Spark That Lit the School

At the start of June, a few Year 8 students noticed the same problem each morning: plastic cups, snack packets and unwanted worksheets were scattered around the courtyard. Complaining had changed nothing, so they started a clean-school campaign. Their first idea was only a small spark. They placed three labelled boxes near the canteen and asked classmates to separate paper, plastic and food waste.

At first, the spark struggled. Some students walked past the boxes, while others dropped rubbish into the wrong container. The campaign team did not give up. They visited classes, demonstrated how to sort waste and posted a weekly “Cleanest Class” result. Their enthusiasm soon caught fire. More students volunteered, teachers joined the lunchtime patrols, and the art club turned old cardboard into bright signs.

Within three weeks, the change could be seen across the school. The courtyard looked clearer, the boxes were used correctly and fewer worksheets were wasted. Even the quietest students carried the message from class to class. What began as one small light had become a line of lanterns.

The campaign did more than remove rubbish. It showed that change does not always begin with a grand plan. Sometimes one useful action lights the next, until an entire community can see the way forward. The Year 8 team did not solve every environmental problem, but they proved that responsibility can spread when people choose to act.

Word help: campaign = organised action for a goal • volunteered = offered to help • responsibility = a duty to act correctly
Think: small beginning → growing excitement/action. Explain how the light/fire image develops.
