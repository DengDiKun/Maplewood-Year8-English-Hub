Step 2 • Prepare properly

Student presentation guide

Follow these instructions in order. Your slides should support your speaking — they should not become a script that you read aloud.

Before you start your slides

1
Prepare a 5–7 minute individual presentation supported by approximately 5–7 slides.
2
Research your topic using at least 2 reliable sources. Read enough to understand the topic instead of copying the first information you find.
3
Your slides support your speaking. Do not write your entire speech on the slides and then read it word-for-word.
4
Your slides should mainly contain headings, short bullet points, photographs or images, useful statistics, short quotations where relevant, and keywords.
Remember: the audience should listen to you, not spend the whole presentation reading paragraphs from your screen.

How to organise your presentation

Slide 1 — Introduction

Show your presentation title. Introduce the topic and explain why you chose it.

Example opening: “Today I am going to talk about… I chose this topic because…”

Slide 2 — Background

Give the important basic information your audience needs before you go deeper.

  • Who or what is it?
  • Where is it from / where did it happen?
  • When did it happen or become important?
  • What is it known for?
Slide 3 — Research Findings

Present the most interesting information you discovered. Include at least 3 important findings. Do more than list simple facts — explain why the information matters.

Slide 4 — Connection to the Learner’s Book

Explain how your research connects to something you studied in Year 8 English.

You may connect it to themes such as strange places, heroes, monsters, the modern world, nature, symbolism, choices or decisions.

Slide 5 — Your Own Analysis

Go beyond reporting facts. Give your own reasoned opinion about the bigger question behind your topic.

Examples: Does this person deserve to be called a hero? Why do people believe this legend? Has this invention improved our lives? Was this decision the right one?

Slide 6 — Conclusion

Briefly summarise what you discovered and explain the one most important idea you want your audience to remember.

Final Slide — Sources

List the websites, books, videos or articles you used for your research.

You do not need to present this slide in detail. It is there to show where your information came from.

Before presentation day

✓
Practise until your presentation fits comfortably within 5–7 minutes.
✓
Know your topic well enough to explain your ideas in your own words.
✓
Expect 1–2 minutes of teacher questions after your presentation. The questions will check whether you genuinely understand your research.
✓
You may use short cue-card keywords if your teacher allows them, but avoid reading a full written script.
A strong presentation: clear research + organised ideas + useful slides + explanation in your own words + confident speaking.
