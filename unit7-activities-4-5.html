Quick recap

Jackson was introduced as an unfriendly and threatening character. He questioned Namidi and behaved aggressively. This increases the tension around the plantation.

Think: Why might the reader already feel that something bad could happen?

Extract 8 — Read carefully

The day had progressed to a burning noon, with the sun gliding overhead like a circle of fire. Fishermen, farmers and women swarmed to the plantation. All around, people fought each other for space around the site.

Namidi stood some distance away, watching over the family possessions while his parents and siblings did their best at the pits.

But the boy wandered about and played with his friend, Onome. They climbed a tree, laughed and swung playfully from branch to branch while the villagers below struggled and argued.

To the children, the fortune-hunters presented entertainment. They giggled at the shouting adults, not understanding the seriousness of what was happening.

swarmed — moved quickly in a large group
sweltering — extremely hot and humid
fortune-hunters — people trying to get rich
tension — a feeling that something bad may happen

Activity 4 — Compare the adults and Ochuko

Simple instruction: Reread the extract. Find evidence for each of these three things.

Sentence starters:
• The adults are ______ because they ______.
• Ochuko is ______ because he ______.
• Ochuko does not understand that ______.
Look for verbs connected to the villagers: fought, struggled, argued, shoved.
Find actions such as played, climbed, laughed, swung, giggled.
Think about the adults’ behaviour and the tension already created by Jackson.

Activity 5 — How does the writer make us worry about Ochuko?

Simple instruction: Write one paragraph explaining how the writer arranges the story to make the reader feel worried for Ochuko.

Use this 4-step paragraph:
1. Point — The writer makes the reader worried by…
2. Evidence — For example, the adults… but Ochuko…
3. Explain — This contrast shows…
4. Effect — The reader feels… because…
Try: “The writer makes the reader feel worried for Ochuko by showing a contrast between the adults and the children…”
