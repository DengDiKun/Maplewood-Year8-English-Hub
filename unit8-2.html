‘The Road Not Taken’

In this poem by Robert Frost, the narrator describes a decision he makes while walking through a wood.

Two roads diverged in a yellow wood, And sorry I could not travel both And be one traveler, long I stood And looked down one as far as I could To where it bent in the undergrowth; Then took the other, as just as fair, And having perhaps the better claim, Because it was grassy and wanted wear; Though as for that the passing there Had worn them really about the same, And both that morning equally lay In leaves no step had trodden black. Oh, I kept the first for another day! Yet knowing how way leads on to way, I doubted if I should ever come back. I shall be telling this with a sigh Somewhere ages and ages hence: Two roads diverged in a wood, and I— I took the one less traveled by, And that has made all the difference.

Note: This text uses American spellings.

‘No Going Back’

Writers often use literal choices to represent important moments in life. Read this short story about a visit to a swimming pool which presents the idea of growing up.

Oliver had been here so many times. As a young boy, he’d stood in the water and looked up, totally in awe at the men diving from the board. As they hurtled down aggressively towards the water, they seemed like eagles, diving with their eyes rigidly fixed and their bodies poised. But today, almost without thinking about it, Oliver found himself steadily climbing the steps up to the diving board. His father was behind him. As he stood on the board, he looked back hesitantly. ‘You can come back if you want,’ said his father. Oliver paused. He wanted to, but some unknown force made him walk to the edge and dive. There was no going back.

Language focus

Adverbs show how a verb action is being done. They can also modify an adjective to give more detail about the manner and extent of an action. Notice how the underlined adverbs in these sentences add detail:

  • He greedily ate the food.
  • She crept up the stairs quietly.
  • I was very angry.
  • I almost fell over.

Adverbs of manner tell you about the way something is done – for example, ‘quickly’, ‘horribly’, ‘fast’, ‘thoughtfully’, ‘incorrectly’.

Adverbs of degree tell you the extent to which something has happened – for example, ‘completely’, ‘quite’, ‘very’, ‘totally’, ‘almost’. Choose adverbs carefully in your writing as they can say a lot about a character’s attitudes and feelings.

Expected answers & strong ideas

These are expected ideas, not the only acceptable wording. Compare them with your own response above.

Activity 2 — Quotation 1
Expected ideas: hesitation, uncertainty and careful thought. The narrator pauses because the choice matters and he wants to consider the possibilities.
Activity 2 — Quotation 2
Expected ideas: uncertainty, realism and possible regret. The narrator understands that one decision may lead to another and he may not be able to return to the original choice.
Activity 2 — Quotation 3
Expected ideas: reflection, acceptance and confidence. The narrator looks back and believes his choice shaped what happened afterwards.
Activity 3 — Feelings
Frost’s narrator is hesitant and reflective. Oliver is nervous and hesitant, but he finally becomes determined enough to dive.
Activity 3 — Metaphors
The two roads can represent different life choices. The diving board and the final dive can represent growing up, taking a risk and committing to a decision.
Activity 3 — Adverbs & structure
‘No Going Back’ uses adverbs such as aggressively, rigidly, steadily, hesitantly and almost to show movement, attitude and uncertainty. Frost uses a regular stanza pattern and reflective ending, while the prose passage builds towards the short final sentence, ‘There was no going back.’
Possible comparison model:
Both texts present important choices as moments of uncertainty. In ‘The Road Not Taken’, the narrator says that he “long I stood”, which shows that he pauses and thinks carefully before choosing a road. Similarly, Oliver “looked back hesitantly” before diving, showing that he is also unsure. However, the poem presents the choice as a metaphor for the direction of a whole life, while ‘No Going Back’ uses the diving board as a symbol of growing up and taking a risk.

The writers also use structure and language to show the effect of a decision. Frost ends by reflecting that his choice “has made all the difference”, suggesting that choices can shape a person’s future. In contrast, the short final sentence “There was no going back” makes Oliver’s decision feel immediate and final. The adverb “hesitantly” also shows his fear before he acts.
